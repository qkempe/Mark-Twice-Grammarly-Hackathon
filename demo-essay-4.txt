The Case for a Four-Day School Week

Across the country, school districts are rethinking the traditional five-day week. Some districts have already made the switch, more are considering it. Supporters say a four-day week could help both students and teachers.

Teachers get an extra day to plan, students get an extra day to rest. Instead of grading papers late on Sunday night, teachers could prepare lessons during the workweek. This makes the job more sustainable.

Honestly, a lot of teachers are super burned out by Friday. A day off gives them time to recover and plan. This is a big reason some teachers choose to stay in four-day districts.

Kids would be way more chill if they had a real break. Everyone knows that rested students learn better. Because the week is shorter, each day runs about an hour longer.

Studies prove that four-day weeks raise test scores everywhere. According to the Pine Valley School District's 2023 attendance report, absences fell by eight percent after the switch. Students can schedule doctor visits on Mondays, they no longer miss class for them.

The schedule does create new problems. Parents who work full time would be kinda stuck finding childcare. This problem is real, and districts should plan for it. This could be solved with community programs on the day off.

Most parents support the change. In a survey of 400 parents in Pine Valley, 61 percent said the new schedule worked for their family. The longer days can be tiring, younger students may struggle to focus in the afternoon.

Some critics think the whole idea is pretty dumb. They argue that students need more time in school, not less. That is why the schedule needs local input. That approach lets each community decide what works.

Pine Valley saved about $200,000 on buses in its first year, and the district used the savings for teacher pay. Experts agree that the four-day week is the future of education. The change is not perfect, it deserves a serious trial.
