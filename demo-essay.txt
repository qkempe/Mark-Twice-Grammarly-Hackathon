Why Schools Should Start Later

Every morning, millions of teenagers drag themselves to school before the sun is up. Doctors say teens need eight to ten hours of sleep, most of them get fewer than seven a night.

The problem is not that teenagers are lazy. During puberty, the body's internal clock shifts later, so most teens cannot fall asleep until about eleven. School often starts at 7:30, many students wake up before six.

Tired students struggle in class. When students sleep more, they miss fewer classes. A sleepy brain has trouble focusing, lessons that should take ten minutes take twenty.

Sleep affects memory, and memory affects learning. During deep sleep, the brain sorts the day's information and stores what matters. Students who skip sleep forget more of what they studied, their grades often drop.

Some districts have tried later start times; however, change has been slow. In Seattle, high schools moved the first bell to 8:45 in 2016. Students there slept about thirty minutes more each night, attendance in first period went up.

Critics worry about bus schedules and after-school sports. These problems are real, they can be solved. If schools start later, students will be healthier, safer, and more ready to learn.
